span

In an HTML document, span represents generic inline container. It works on a small piece of text inside a sentence, like placing a useful label or emphasis around selected words.

Anything meant to belong to span normally sits inside the pair of tags. Keep the opening tag before the affected words and the closing tag immediately after them.

This short sample shows span in use:

<p>
  Add the <span class="ingredient">basil</span>,
  <span class="ingredient">pine nuts</span> and
  <span class="ingredient">garlic</span> to a blender and blend into a paste.
</p>

<p>
  Gradually add the <span class="ingredient">olive oil</span> while running the
  blender slowly.
</p>

What this example does: Start with the span tag in the code, then inspect what sits inside it and which attributes have been added to its opening tag.
