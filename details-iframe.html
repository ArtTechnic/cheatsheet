iframe

Use iframe when a piece of HTML represents embedded browsing context. It places another resource or browsing area inside the current page, like making a window that looks into something else.

Anything meant to belong to iframe normally sits inside the pair of tags. Embedded material can affect security, speed, and accessibility, so include only resources you trust and actually need.

Important: This feature is experimental. Check browser support before relying on it.

Use this iframe sample as a starting point, then change one piece at a time:

<iframe
  id="inlineFrameExample"
  title="Inline Frame Example"
  width="300"
  height="200"
  src="https://www.openstreetmap.org/export/embed.html?bbox=-0.004017949104309083%2C51.47612752641776%2C0.00030577182769775396%2C51.478569861898606&amp;layer=mapnik">
</iframe>

What to notice: The sample demonstrates <iframe>.
