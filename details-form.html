form

Use form when a piece of HTML represents form container. It is a form building block: forms let a visitor enter information, choose something, or start an action.

Treat the two form tags like the covers of a folder: the related content goes between them. Every control needs a clear label, and the page should still make sense when somebody uses only a keyboard.

Important: This is an older feature. You may meet it in old websites, but you should normally choose a modern alternative for new work.

Here is a compact form example you can inspect and alter:

<form action="" method="get" class="form-example">
  <div class="form-example">
    <label for="name">Enter your name: </label>
    <input type="text" name="name" id="name" required />
  </div>
  <div class="form-example">
    <label for="email">Enter your email: </label>
    <input type="email" name="email" id="email" required />
  </div>
  <div class="form-example">
    <input type="submit" value="Subscribe!" />
  </div>
</form>

The important part: The key piece in this sample is <form>. Read outward from that tag to see what belongs to it and how it is configured.
