th

A th (table header) element creates a header cell. Header cells label the rows or columns of your table. In the example at the top, "Project" and "State" are header cells because they tell you what the data below means. By default, browsers make th text bold and centered so it stands out.

There is an important attribute called scope that goes with th. It tells assistive technology (like screen readers) whether the header applies to a column (scope="col") or a row (scope="row"). Without it, a screen reader might read the table in a confusing order. Always add scope to your th cells so everyone can follow along.

The example below shows header cells with scope attributes. Notice how the top row uses scope="col" and the leftmost cells in the body use scope="row".



<tr>
    <th scope="col">Project</th>
    <th scope="col">State</th>
</tr>
<tr>
    <th scope="row">Cheat sheet</th>
    <td>Active</td>
</tr>
<tr>
    <th scope="row">Todo list</th>
    <td>Done</td>
</tr>
