HTML

a

Use a when a piece of HTML represents hyperlink. Think of it as a signpost: part of the code says where something is, and the browser uses that address to reach it.

Anything meant to belong to a normally sits inside the pair of tags. Check the address carefully. A small spelling mistake can point to a missing page or resource.

Important: This is an older feature. You may meet it in old websites, but you should normally choose a modern alternative for new work.

This short sample shows a in use:

<p>You can reach Michael at:</p>

<ul>
  <li><a href="https://example.com">Website</a></li>
  <li><a href="mailto:m.bluth@example.com">Email</a></li>
  <li><a href="tel:+123456789">Phone</a></li>
</ul>

What this example does: This sample puts a into a small piece of realistic HTML. Change its content or one attribute at a time and watch what differs.
