header

Use header when a piece of HTML represents introductory content. It identifies what kind of section this is, rather like putting a label on a folder before placing papers inside it.

Treat the two header tags like the covers of a folder: the related content goes between them. Choose it because the content has that meaning. If you only need an unlabelled box for styling, a div may be the clearer choice.

The code below shows a simple use of header:

<header>
  <a class="logo" href="#">Cute Puppies Express!</a>
</header>

<article>
  <header>
    <h1>Beagles</h1>
    <time>08.12.2014</time>
  </header>
  <p>
    I love beagles <em>so</em> much! Like, really, a lot. They're adorable and
    their ears are so, so snugly soft!
  </p>
</article>

What to notice: This sample puts header into a small piece of realistic HTML. Change its content or one attribute at a time and watch what differs.
